# Walmart Project 1 — Edition B Investment Committee Memo

**Status:** Draft for committee review; Watch–Defer is provisional, not a final recommendation.  
**Proposed valuation date:** September 23, 2026  
**Market reference:** WMT closing price $110.53 on September 23, 2026 (historical-price source cited below).  
**Units:** USD millions unless stated otherwise.

## Executive recommendation

Maintain **Watch–Defer provisionally**. The available analysis does not support initiating at the reported $110.53 close: the causal V3 DCF cases indicate $32.40–$44.00 per share, Target-based indications are $44.74–$49.24, and the conditional screening zone is $31–$49. At the observed price, the reverse DCF requires operating outcomes far above the V3 base and upside cases. This supports deferring a purchase pending stronger evidence, but it does **not** establish a fully validated intrinsic value or prove that the market price is wrong.

The conclusion is conditional because the dominant DCF inputs are not verified for September 23: WACC, diluted shares, cash available to equity, debt value, finance-lease claims, and parts of operating working capital. More than 81% of scenario enterprise value is terminal value. Internal accounting checks pass, but they test model mechanics—not source completeness, forecast truth, or valuation-date alignment. Accordingly, the $31–$49 zone is a decision aid, not a statistically derived fair-value range or an automatic buy/sell threshold.

## Operating and investment thesis

Walmart's reported FY2026 net sales were $706.413 billion, membership and other income $6.750 billion, total revenues $713.163 billion, and operating income $29.825 billion. The business thesis is that its scale, broad merchandise assortment, store and digital footprint, grocery traffic, membership, advertising and fulfillment capabilities can support resilient sales and gradually improve operating productivity. Those are investment hypotheses, not independently established drivers of the forecast.

The latest pre-valuation-date Walmart interim filing in the evidence set is the FY2027 second-quarter Form 10-Q for the six months ended July 31, 2026, filed August 28: reported total revenue was $365.688 billion versus $343.011 billion in the comparable prior period, and operating income was $16.876 billion versus $14.421 billion. These reported aggregates provide context but do not by themselves establish the next five years' growth, margin, investment, or cash-conversion paths.

The central debate is valuation versus expectations. The V3 operating cases do not reach the market price, while the reverse solve needs either exceptionally high sustained growth or a step-change in expense efficiency. A durable acceleration in digital, membership, advertising and productivity could help support higher expectations; weaker consumer demand, price competition, labor/supply-chain costs, reinvestment, or working-capital demands could instead pressure FCFF.

| Value per share | **$36.7804** | Equity value ÷ August 26 basic shares |

The bridge convention is enterprise value plus excess cash and eligible nonoperating assets, less debt-like claims and noncontrolling claims, divided by a consistent diluted equity denominator. V3's zero NCI/investment adjustments and cash-as-excess treatment are provisional. The July 31 cash, borrowings, and lease amounts predate the valuation date. Walmart's FY2026 10-K also discusses cash balances not freely transferable; that disclosure does not establish the restricted/excess split on September 23. Operating-lease treatment must remain consistent between FCFF/enterprise value and the equity bridge to avoid double counting; the current peer EV analysis excludes lease liabilities while V3 separately deducts finance leases, so the comparison is not perfectly aligned.

The 8,022 million FY2026 weighted-average diluted shares reported for earnings are a period average, not a point-in-time valuation denominator. Conversely, the 7,933.746241 million August 26 figure is basic shares and is 28 days before the proposed valuation date. Neither has been demonstrated to be the correct September 23 diluted claim count.

## WACC, terminal value, and conditional range

V3 uses a **7.00% WACC** and **2.50% terminal growth**, both provisional. The prior preliminary 6.6774% is an internally calculated result from an older assumption set, not a verified rate. A separate 7.6748% calculation is illustrative only and uses a 5.11% 10-year Treasury observation, 0.55 unverified beta, 5.00% published ERP, a 5.66% AA corporate-yield proxy for debt cost, 24.5% tax, and mixed-date estimated capital weights. The Kroll September 2026 ERP recommendation pairs with a spot 20-year Treasury rate, so the 10-year/ERP combination is not methodologically aligned. Neither 6.6774%, 7.00%, nor 7.6748% is selected as a final WACC.

At 7.00% WACC and 2.50% growth, V3 base is $36.78/share; at illustrative 7.6748% WACC and the same growth it is about $31.19/share. Terminal value is approximately 82% of base-case EV. Beta, ERP application, company-specific cost of debt, tax rate, market-value weights, and valuation-date capital values remain unresolved, making this sensitivity especially important.

For committee discussion only, **$31–$49 per share is a conditional screening zone**: its low end rounds the V3 base under the illustrative higher WACC; its high end rounds Target's EV/EBIT indication using the V3 bridge. It is not a statistically derived interval, does not resolve methodological differences, and is not a final fair-value conclusion. The three causal cases at the model's 7.00% rate are $32.40–$44.00. The range does not include Costco's premium indications as a central anchor.

## Reverse DCF versus the $110.53 market price

StockAnalysis historical prices report a $110.53 WMT close on September 23, 2026; this is a secondary market-data source, not a primary exchange record. Under V3's fixed WACC, terminal growth, bridge and share basis, the base model produces $36.78, $73.75 below the close. The reverse DCF can reproduce $110.53 in at least two distinct ways:

- Raise annual growth in both net sales and membership/other income by 11.1845 percentage points, yielding 15.4845%, 15.3845%, 15.1845%, 14.9845%, and 14.6845% through FY2031. This implies about 15.14% five-year revenue CAGR and $1.443 trillion FY2031 revenue.
- Alternatively, reduce cash operating expense / operating gross profit ratios by 17.1921 percentage points, to about 58.01%–57.31%, implying EBIT margins near 8.9%.

Both paths reproduce the price arithmetically but are not unique forecasts or verified market expectations; terminal value is 81%–86% of EV in the solves. Sustained growth or margin evidence could make the price easier to support. Results showing materially slower growth, margins failing to expand, higher capital needs, or weaker working-capital conversion would falsify these illustrative paths. Their exact solved values are conditional on provisional WACC, bridge and shares.

## Risks, uncertainties, and falsification questions

Key risks include consumer and category demand, price competition and gross-margin execution, labor and fulfillment costs, digital/membership monetization, capex burden, inventory and supplier terms, claims/contingencies, debt and liquidity needs, and sensitivity to discount rate and terminal assumptions. Quantitatively, terminal value dominates the DCF. The highest operating-driver uncertainty is the unsupported $9.022 billion upside-case FY2027 working-capital release.

Questions that could falsify the thesis or the modeled cases:

- Do net sales and membership/other income grow differently from the common V3 path, and can the business sustain the modeled gross-margin and opex-ratio improvement?
- Is working-capital efficiency durable after separating supplier-finance arrangements and operating from financing/nonoperating balances?
- Does growth require greater capex, lease commitments, or labor investment than modeled?
- What portion of reported cash is available to equity holders, and what are the September 23 debt-like claims, finance leases, NCI and nonoperating investments?
- Can a source-supported WACC and a date-consistent diluted share count be established, and does the price still materially exceed a refreshed value?
- Do Target's tariff refund and peer date/lease differences materially change the apparent comp reference?

## Watch, initiate, or do not initiate conditions

**Initiate consideration** only after a refreshed, source-backed valuation-date bridge and share denominator, defensible WACC, consistent lease treatment, and a documented margin-of-safety rule are available—and reported operating evidence supports the selected scenario. No margin-of-safety threshold is assumed in this memo.

**Remain on Watch–Defer** while key source gaps remain, the market price is materially above the conditional zone, or upcoming disclosures could reasonably validate or reject the growth/margin and working-capital hypotheses. This is the current provisional state.

**Do not initiate / defer further** if the investment case at the prevailing price depends on the reverse-DCF growth or efficiency path without supporting evidence, if downside liquidity approaches the modeled revolver limit, or if source-verified bridge/WACC changes reduce supportable value further. This describes a decision condition, not a permanent judgment on Walmart.

## Research plan and monitoring triggers

Before final submission, reconcile valuation-date shares, cash, debt, finance leases, noncontrolling interests and nonoperating investments to dated primary records; establish company-specific debt yield and market-value capital weights; validate the risk-free/ERP maturity pairing, beta methodology and tax rate; and document operating working-capital accounts and supplier-finance treatment. Re-run the unchanged V3 mechanics only after documenting approved input changes; preserve all historical versions and label model controls separately from source checks.

At each quarterly filing, monitor net sales separately from membership/other income, comparable sales, digital and membership contribution where reported, gross margin, operating expense, capex, inventory/payables/receivables, cash, borrowings, finance leases, liquidity and share counts. Revisit the reverse DCF when actual results materially converge toward or diverge from the implied growth or margin paths. Rebuild peer multiples from same-date prices and latest available consistent filings; isolate exceptional items such as Target's tariff-refund benefit. The $31–$49 conditional zone is a review trigger only, not a mechanical trade rule.

2. Establish September 23 point-in-time diluted shares, reconciling the August 26 basic count and FY2026 weighted-average diluted count without substituting one for the other by assumption.
3. Obtain date-consistent cash and debt amounts and define excess/restricted cash and debt market value; resolve the July 31 bridge timing.
4. Reconcile finance-lease and operating-lease claims across forecast FCFF, enterprise value, debt measure and peer multiples; test for double counting.
5. Determine noncontrolling-interest and nonoperating-investment treatment using supportable valuation-date amounts rather than zero by default.
6. Validate individual working-capital classifications, supplier financing, accrued/prepaid and tax balances, and the large upside release; do not infer operating status from similar labels.
7. Support or revise the common growth path for net sales and membership/other income, margins, capex, cash taxes and financing assumptions; assess whether full fiscal-year discounting is appropriate for a September 23 valuation date.
8. Improve peer comparability, including lease policy, exceptional items, share and balance dates, and the small two-company sample; then state an explicit margin-of-safety and decision rule.

Until these items are resolved and independently reviewed, this memo and all scenario values remain provisional decision-support materials—not a fully validated valuation or final investment conclusion.

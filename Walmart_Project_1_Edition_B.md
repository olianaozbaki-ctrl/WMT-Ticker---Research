# Walmart Inc. — Company Research Report

## Research decision

This report evaluates whether a long-term, fundamentals-oriented equity investor should begin a valuation-driven research position in Walmart Inc., keep the company on a watch list, or stop the work. The information cutoff and call date are March 13, 2026, the filing date of Walmart's fiscal 2026 Form 10-K. [Source: SEC EDGAR filing detail, March 13, 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/104169/000010416926000055/0000104169-26-000055-index.html)

My initial judgment is **watch-defer**. Walmart's operating performance is strong enough to justify continued research, but the 10-K alone does not establish that the shares should be purchased. Consolidated net sales and Walmart U.S. comparable sales grew in fiscal 2026, while the consolidated gross-profit rate improved slightly. [Source: Walmart 2026 Form 10-K, Item 7, “Company Performance Metrics—Growth” and “Margin,” pp. 34–35](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/104169/000010416926000055/wmt-20260131.htm) At the same time, operating expenses grew faster than sales, and operating margin declined to 4.2% from 4.4%. [Source: Walmart 2026 Form 10-K, Item 7, “Company Performance Metrics—Margin,” pp. 34–35](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/104169/000010416926000055/wmt-20260131.htm) Before initiating a position, I need a market-price-based valuation and a clearer view of the returns Walmart can earn on its large capital program. [Source for the size and allocation of the capital program: Walmart 2026 Form 10-K, Item 7, “Company Performance Metrics—Capital Allocation,” p. 36](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/104169/000010416926000055/wmt-20260131.htm)

## Operating evidence

Walmart reported fiscal 2026 net sales of $706.413 billion, an increase of 4.7% from fiscal 2025. [Source: Walmart 2026 Form 10-K, Item 7, “Results of Operations—Consolidated Results of Operations,” pp. 37–38](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/104169/000010416926000055/wmt-20260131.htm) Operating income was $29.825 billion. [Source: Walmart 2026 Form 10-K, Item 7, “Results of Operations—Consolidated Results of Operations,” pp. 37–38](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/104169/000010416926000055/wmt-20260131.htm)

The gross-profit rate was 24.2% in fiscal 2026, compared with 24.1% in fiscal 2025. [Source: Walmart 2026 Form 10-K, Item 7, “Company Performance Metrics—Margin,” pp. 34–35](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/104169/000010416926000055/wmt-20260131.htm) Operating income was 4.2% of net sales, down from 4.4% in fiscal 2025. [Source: Walmart 2026 Form 10-K, Item 7, “Company Performance Metrics—Margin,” pp. 34–35](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/104169/000010416926000055/wmt-20260131.htm)

My initial operating-value driver is **Walmart U.S. comparable-sales growth**. Walmart U.S. comparable sales increased 4.3% in fiscal 2026. [Source: Walmart 2026 Form 10-K, Item 7, “Company Performance Metrics—Growth,” p. 34](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/104169/000010416926000055/wmt-20260131.htm) Management attributed the increase to growth in average ticket and transactions, along with unit-volume growth and strength across merchandise categories. [Source: Walmart 2026 Form 10-K, Item 7, “Results of Operations—Walmart U.S. Segment,” pp. 38–39](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/104169/000010416926000055/wmt-20260131.htm) Walmart U.S. eCommerce contributed approximately 4.3 percentage points to comparable sales, primarily through store-fulfilled pickup and delivery and continued engagement with Walmart's omnichannel offerings. [Source: Walmart 2026 Form 10-K, Item 7, “Results of Operations—Walmart U.S. Segment,” pp. 38–39](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/104169/000010416926000055/wmt-20260131.htm)

These results support continued research because sales growth was not limited to pricing: management also reported transaction and unit-volume growth. [Source for the operating facts: Walmart 2026 Form 10-K, Item 7, “Results of Operations—Walmart U.S. Segment,” pp. 38–39](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/104169/000010416926000055/wmt-20260131.htm) My concern is that this demand evidence has not yet translated into consolidated operating-margin expansion.

## Investment case and consequential assumptions

My watch-defer judgment depends on the following assumptions:

- Comparable-sales and eCommerce momentum can continue without requiring price investment that materially weakens margins.
- Productivity and growth in higher-margin businesses can eventually offset higher depreciation and other operating-cost pressure.
- Walmart's elevated capital investment will earn an adequate return rather than mainly raising depreciation and maintenance needs.
- Fiscal 2026 contains enough representative operating information to form a starting earnings base, subject to normalization for nonrecurring gains, charges, and unusual expenses.
- The eventual enterprise-to-equity bridge will treat debt, leases, investments, noncontrolling interests, and other claims consistently with the selected valuation method.

These are analytical assumptions, not established facts. They are the conditions that must be tested before I would change the call.

## Important unknowns

This report does not yet resolve:

- Walmart's market price and implied valuation multiples on the March 13, 2026 valuation date.
- The sustainable margin and cash contribution from eCommerce, advertising, marketplace, fulfillment, and other higher-margin businesses.
- The portion of current capital spending that is maintenance versus growth, and the timing and size of its return.
- Whether higher claims expense and other recent cost pressures are temporary or persistent.
- The appropriate valuation treatment of leases, noncontrolling interests, investments, and other non-operating claims.
- The sensitivity of revenue, margin, and working capital to tariffs, product mix, consumer pressure, and competitive price investment.

Because the original report provided no additional sources addressing these questions, any assertion that resolves them would be **UNSOURCED**.

## Research required before changing the call

1. Build a five-year operating history from Walmart's SEC filings, separating sales growth, comparable sales, margins, working capital, capital spending, and free cash flow.
2. Reconcile diluted shares and every enterprise-to-equity bridge item, including leases, investments, and noncontrolling interests.
3. Study segment and business-mix economics, with particular attention to eCommerce, advertising, marketplace, membership, and fulfillment.
4. Normalize fiscal 2026 earnings and cash flow for unusual gains, charges, and expenses disclosed in the filing.
5. Create base, upside, and downside forecasts and evaluate them with a discounted cash-flow analysis and relevant trading multiples using market data as of the valuation date.
6. Test the analysis against the principal risks and the falsification question before changing the initial conclusion.

The key falsification question is: **What could cause Walmart's sales and profits to decline in the future?**

## Final call

**Call as of March 13, 2026: WATCH-DEFER.**

I would change this call to **initiate-buy** if a market-price-based valuation showed an attractive return while further research demonstrated that Walmart's growth, margin improvement, and capital-investment returns were sustainable; I would change it to **do-not-initiate** if that research instead showed inadequate prospective returns or that the operating thesis was breaking down.

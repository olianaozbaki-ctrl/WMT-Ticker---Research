## R — Meridian Foundation Case

S1 — Hard Constraint
The portfolio has a seven-year investment horizon and needs to be rebalanced annually.

S2 — Objective
The foundation wants to earn a return of CPI + 4% annually after costs over rolling five-year periods.

S3 — Hard Constraint
The foundation needs enough cash or eligible government securities to cover twelve months of quarterly distributions.

S4 — Preference
The 60% equity and 40% bond benchmark provides a reference for comparing portfolio performance rather than a required allocation.

S5 — Hard Constraint
The portfolio cannot use borrowing, short selling, or derivatives.

S6 — Hard Constraint
No single issuer can account for more than 8% of the portfolio.

S7 — Hard Constraint
Annual turnover cannot exceed 25% without committee approval.

S8 — Hard Constraint
At least 90% of the portfolio must be redeemable within five business days.

S9 — Unverifiable Promise
The foundation wants to avoid annual losses greater than 10%, but this cannot be guaranteed and the CIO has not defined how a breach would be measured or handled.

S10 — Missing
The CIO has not specified what the portfolio should optimize, so more information is needed before selecting the best portfolio.

## E — Candidate D

Candidate D consists of 3% cash and 97% Treasury 10-year notes. It passes every checkable rule in the Python checker.

I think the CIO's objection is reasonable because even though Candidate D passes the checks, having 97% invested in 10-year Treasury notes could lead to significant losses if interest rates rise. This relates to S9 because the foundation wants to avoid calendar-year losses greater than 10%, but the statement does not clearly define how that risk should be measured or handled.

## Question for the CIO

Question: What should the portfolio prioritize when optimizing: maximizing returns or minimizing risk while meeting the foundation's return objective?

How the answer changes my portfolio: If the CIO prioritizes minimizing risk, I would focus more on investments with lower volatility while still working toward the return objective. If maximizing returns is the priority, I would consider a greater allocation to equities, as long as the portfolio continues to meet the foundation's constraints.

## Investment Policy Statement (IPS)

An Investment Policy Statement outlines an investor's goals, risk tolerance, and restrictions to help guide investment decisions. For the Meridian Foundation, the statements can be organized into objectives and constraints.

Objectives:
- S2: Achieve a return of CPI + 4% annually after costs.
- S4: Use the 60/40 benchmark to evaluate portfolio performance.
- S9: Limit exposure to large calendar-year losses, although this needs further clarification.

Constraints:
- S1: Seven-year investment horizon with annual rebalancing.
- S3: Maintain enough reserves for twelve months of distributions.
- S5: No borrowing, short selling, or derivatives.
- S6: Limit exposure to a single issuer to 8%.
- S7: Keep annual turnover below 25% without approval.
- S8: Maintain at least 90% liquidity within five business days.

Missing Information:
- S10: The CIO needs to clarify the portfolio's optimization objective before making a final investment decision.

## Reflection

A portfolio can pass every checkable rule and still be a poor investment choice because the Python checker only evaluates specific constraints, not whether the portfolio will actually meet the foundation's long-term goals. Candidate D is a good example because it passes all the checks but could still experience significant losses if interest rates rise.

The hardest statements to classify were those related to risk, especially S9. It was difficult because the foundation wants to avoid losses greater than 10%, but this cannot be guaranteed or properly tested without more clarification from the CIO.
